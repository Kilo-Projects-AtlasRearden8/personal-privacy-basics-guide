Locking down your phone

Your phone knows where you are, who you talk to, what you buy, and often what you look like. Losing it — or having someone get into it — is a big deal. This chapter walks through locking it down, step by step. Steps work for both iPhone (iOS) and Android; where they differ, both are shown.

Step 1: Lock the screen

  1. Set your phone to lock automatically after a short time — 30 seconds to 2 minutes.
  2. Use a strong passcode: six digits or more, or an alphanumeric passcode. Avoid simple patterns like 123456 or 000000.
  3. Turn on Face ID, Touch ID, or fingerprint unlock if your phone has it. Biometrics are convenient for daily use; your passcode is still the backup.

iPhone — Settings → Face ID & Passcode (or Touch ID & Passcode) → Require Passcode → choose Immediately or After 30 seconds.

Android — Settings → Security → Screen lock → choose PIN or Password (6+ digits). Then Settings → Security → Screen timeout → pick a short time.

Step 2: Turn on “Find my phone”

If you lose your phone, this lets you locate it, lock it, or erase it from another device.

iPhone — Settings → [your name] → Find My → Find My iPhone → turn on Find My iPhone and Send Last Location.

Android — Settings → Security → Find My Device (or Settings → Google → Find My Device) → turn it on.
Then test it: go to android.com/find from a computer and sign in with the same Google account.

Step 3: Control app permissions

Apps ask to access your location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone, and more. Many ask for more than they need.

How to review (iPhone) — Settings → Privacy & Security. Tap each category (Location Services, Contacts, Photos, Camera, Microphone) and remove access from apps that do not need it.
For Location Services, you can set apps to “While Using the App” or “Never” instead of “Always.”

How to review (Android) — Settings → Privacy → Permission manager. Tap each permission and remove it from apps that do not need it.
Also: Settings → Apps → [app name] → Permissions.

Rule of thumb: if a flashlight app wants your contacts, say no. If a map app wants location “Always,” consider “While using the app” instead.

Step 4: Keep your phone and apps updated

Updates fix security holes attackers use to get into phones.

  1. Turn on automatic OS updates.
    iPhone: Settings → General → Software Update → Automatic Updates → turn on all options.
    Android: Settings → System → Advanced → System update (varies by phone).
  2. Turn on automatic app updates.
    iPhone: Settings → App Store → App Updates → on.
    Android: Play Store → profile icon → Settings → Auto-update apps → Over any network.
  3. When an update is available, install it promptly.

Step 5: Be careful on public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi (coffee shops, airports, hotels) can let others on the same network see what you are doing on unencrypted sites.

Safer habits

  • Avoid banking, shopping, or typing passwords on public Wi-Fi.
  • Use your phone’s cellular data for sensitive tasks instead.
  • If you must use public Wi-Fi, use a reputable VPN app. A VPN encrypts the connection between your phone and the VPN server.
    Do not use free VPNs from unknown companies; many sell your data. Pick a well-known paid service or one built into your operating system.
  • Turn off “Auto-join” for networks you do not trust.
    iPhone: Settings → Wi-Fi → tap the (i) next to a network → turn off Auto-Join.
    Android: Settings → Network & internet → Internet → tap the network → turn off automatic reconnect.

Step 6: Review what your phone shares

Location

  • iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → System Services → review “Significant Locations” and turn it off if you do not want your phone remembering places you go.
  • Android: Settings → Location → review app permissions and Google location history settings.

Advertising ID

Phones carry an advertising ID that lets apps show you targeted ads. You can reset or limit it.

  • iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking → turn off “Allow Apps to Request to Track.”
  • Android: Settings → Privacy → Ads → opt out of ads personalization (or delete your advertising ID).

Step 7: Lock your SIM card

Someone who takes your SIM card can put it in another phone and receive your texts — including two-factor codes sent by text.

Set a SIM PIN so the SIM cannot be used in another phone without unlocking it.

  • iPhone: Settings → Cellular → SIM PIN → turn on (you will need the default PIN from your carrier; after you set it, the phone asks for it on restart).
  • Android: Settings → Security → SIM card lock → Lock SIM card.
Warning: if you enter the wrong SIM PIN three times, the SIM locks and you need a PUK code from your carrier to unlock it. Only turn this on if you are comfortable keeping that code safe.

Step 8: Set up remote erase

If your phone is stolen, you can erase everything on it from afar.

  • iPhone: Find My app → Devices → [your phone] → Erase This Device.
  • Android: android.com/find → [your phone] → Erase device.

Erasing is permanent. Make sure your photos and data are backed up first (iCloud, Google One, or your computer).

Quick checklist for this chapter

  • Screen lock with 6+ digit passcode or alphanumeric passcode
  • Auto-lock set to 30 seconds–2 minutes
  • Find My iPhone / Find My Device turned on and tested
  • App permissions reviewed (location, contacts, camera, microphone)
  • Phone OS and apps set to update automatically
  • Public Wi-Fi avoided for banking/shopping (or VPN used)
  • Ad tracking limited / advertising ID reset
  • SIM PIN set (optional, keep PUK code safe)
  • Remote erase set up; phone backed up
