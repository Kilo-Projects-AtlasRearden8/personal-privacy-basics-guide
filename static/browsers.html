Locking down your browser

Your browser is where most of your online life happens — shopping, banking, reading, searching. Browsers also collect a lot about you: the sites you visit, what you click, where you are, and sometimes what you type. This chapter shows you how to tighten your browser, step by step.

Every name, email, and address in this guide is made up.

Step 1: Pick a privacy-friendly browser

Some browsers collect less data by default. Good options include:

  • Firefox (Mozilla) — strong privacy controls, not made by an advertising company
  • Brave — blocks ads and trackers by default
  • Safari (Apple) — strong tracker blocking on Mac and iPhone
  • Microsoft Edge — has tracking prevention options (turn them on)

If you use Chrome, you can still harden it (steps below), but it is made by Google, an advertising company, and collects more data by default.

Step 2: Turn on tracking protection

Trackers are little pieces of code on websites that follow you from site to site and build a profile of you.

  • Firefox — Open Settings → Privacy & Security. Under “Enhanced Tracking Protection,” choose Strict. If a site breaks, lower protection for that site only (click the shield icon in the address bar).
  • Safari — Open Settings (or Preferences) → Privacy. Turn on Prevent cross-site tracking. Turn on Hide IP address from trackers if offered.
  • Brave — Tracking protection is on by default. Check Settings → Shields to make sure it is active.
  • Edge — Settings → Privacy, search, and services. Set Tracking prevention to Balanced or Strict.
  • Chrome — Settings → Privacy and security → Third-party cookies. Choose Block third-party cookies. Turn on Send a “Do Not Track” request with your browsing traffic (note: many sites ignore this, but it costs nothing).

Step 3: Block ads and trackers with an extension

An extension adds extra protection on top of your browser’s built-in settings.

  1. Go to your browser’s add-on store (Firefox Add-ons, Chrome Web Store, etc.).
  2. Search for uBlock Origin. Install the one made by Raymond Hill (the official one; there are copycats).
  3. After installing, click the extension icon and make sure it is turned on for the sites you visit.

uBlock Origin blocks ads, trackers, and many known scam or malware sites. It is free and open source.

Step 4: Browse privately when you do not want history saved

Private browsing (called “Private Window” in Firefox/Safari, “Incognito” in Chrome) means:

  • The browser does not save your history or cookies after you close the window.
  • Websites can still see your IP address.
  • Your employer, school, or internet provider can still see where you go.

Use it for: looking up a gift, checking bank balance on a shared computer, searching something you do not want in your main history.

Do not use it thinking it makes you invisible. It does not.

Step 5: Clear cookies and history regularly

Cookies let sites remember you, but they also let trackers follow you.

How to clear (Firefox example)

  1. Settings → Privacy & Security → Cookies and Site Data.
  2. Click Clear Data. Check “Cookies and Site Data” and click Clear.

You can also set Firefox to “Delete cookies and site data when Firefox is closed” under the same menu.

Other browsers have similar options under Settings → Privacy → Clear browsing data.

Step 6: Be careful signing into your browser

Browsers can sync your history, passwords, and bookmarks across devices using your Google, Apple, or Firefox account.

  • This is convenient, but it means that company stores a copy of your browsing data.
  • If you sync, protect that account with a strong password and 2FA (see the accounts chapter).
  • Use a strong sync passphrase if your browser offers one (Firefox and Chrome support encrypting synced data with a password only you know).
  • Do not sign into your browser on a shared or public computer. If you do, sign out and remove the profile before you leave.

Step 7: Look before you click

  1. Hover over a link before clicking. The real destination usually appears at the bottom of the browser window.
  2. Watch for tricky addresses: paypa1.com (number one instead of l) is not paypal.com.
  3. Type the address yourself for banking or shopping instead of clicking a link in an email or text.

Step 8: Keep your browser updated

Updates fix security holes that attackers use.

  • Most browsers update automatically. Restart your browser when it asks.
  • On mobile, update apps through your phone’s app store.

Quick checklist for this chapter

  • Privacy-friendly browser chosen (or current browser hardened)
  • Tracking protection turned on (Strict/Balanced)
  • uBlock Origin (or similar blocker) installed
  • Third-party cookies blocked
  • Private browsing used when needed
  • Cookies/history cleared (or set to clear automatically)
  • Browser sync protected with strong password + 2FA (if used)
  • Browser set to update automatically
