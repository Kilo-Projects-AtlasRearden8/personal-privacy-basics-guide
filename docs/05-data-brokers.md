# Opting out of data brokers

Data brokers are companies that collect and sell information about people: addresses, phone numbers, relatives, property records, court records, and sometimes browsing habits. They get this data from public records, social media, store loyalty programs, and other companies. Sites like people-search pages then display it, often for free or a small fee.

This chapter explains how to find your information on data broker sites and ask them to remove it.

Every name, address, phone number, and email in this guide is made up.

## Step 1: See what is out there

1. Search your name in quotes in a search engine: `"Jane Doe"` (use your real name when you do this for yourself; `Jane Doe` here is just an example).
2. Add your city or state: `"Jane Doe" Springfield`.
3. Look for people-search sites in the results. Common ones include sites that show addresses, phone numbers, relatives, or background reports.

Do not pay for a background report on yourself just to see what is there. Free search results usually show enough to know which sites have your info.

## Step 2: Keep a list

Write down each site that shows your information, the URL of your listing page, and the date you checked. You will need this to send opt-out requests and to check back later.

Example list (fake entries):

| Site | Listing URL | Date checked | Opt-out sent |
|------|-------------|--------------|--------------|
| Example People Search | people.example/jane-doe-123 | 2026-10-01 | Yes |
| Example Data Co | dataco.example/profile/998 | 2026-10-01 | No |

Never post this list publicly. It contains personal information about you.

## Step 3: Opt out of each site

Most data brokers have an opt-out or "do not sell my info" page. The process usually looks like:

1. Find the opt-out link (often at the bottom of the site in tiny text: "Opt out," "Remove my info," "Privacy," or "Do not sell my personal information").
2. Find your listing and copy its URL.
3. Fill out the opt-out form with your name and the listing URL. Some sites ask for an email to confirm — consider using an email address you do not use widely, since you may get confirmation emails.
4. Confirm the opt-out by clicking the link in the confirmation email.
5. Record the date in your list.

Some sites require you to mail or fax a request. Others make it intentionally hard. Do your best; removing what you can still helps.

### California, Colorado, Virginia, and some other states

If you live in California (CCPA), Colorado (CPA), Virginia (VCDPA), or certain other states, you have a legal right to ask companies not to sell your personal information. Look for a "Do Not Sell My Personal Information" link on websites and use it. It applies to many data brokers and other companies, not just people-search sites.

## Step 4: Use an opt-out service (optional, costs money)

Doing this yourself is free but time-consuming — there are hundreds of brokers. Paid services (for example, DeleteMe, Optery, Incogni) send opt-out requests for you and keep checking. They typically cost between $60 and $130 per year.

Think of it like hiring someone to do paperwork: convenient, not magic. They cannot remove everything, and you should still check back yourself once or twice a year.

## Step 5: Check back regularly

Data brokers relist information when new public records appear.

- Re-search your name every 3–6 months.
- Repeat the opt-out process for any site that relists you.
- Update your list each time.

## Step 6: Reduce what brokers can collect going forward

1. **Limit what you share publicly.** Avoid posting your full birth date, home address, phone number, or relatives' names on social media.
2. **Opt out of marketing lists.** On accounts you use, look for marketing/email preferences and turn off "share my data with partners."
3. **Use variations.** For loyalty cards, newsletters, or contests, consider using a dedicated email address (not your main one) and, where allowed, a name variation.
4. **Remove old accounts.** Delete shopping, forum, and newsletter accounts you no longer use. Less data exists to be scraped.

## What removal does and does not do

| Does | Does not |
|------|----------|
| Removes your listing from that broker's public site | Remove data from government public records (court filings, property records) |
| Reduce how easily strangers can find your address/phone | Guarantee permanent removal (brokers may relist you) |
| Apply to that broker only | Remove your data from every broker at once |
